Section 1 of 11

What this is

A place to keep every reservation you have sold, chase the money on time, and know what you are owed. It is the book of business that usually lives in a spreadsheet, an inbox and somebody's memory.

One thing it is not: it does not take money. Your client pays the cruise line, the tour operator or the resort, the same as they always did. The portal records what is owed and when, reminds you and them before the date, and tells you what the vendor still owes you afterwards. Nothing here touches a card.

Learning it, and looking things up

Training is the whole portal taught in the order you would meet it: a first week that builds one real reservation, then each part of the menu in turn, what it is for and the thing people get wrong. Nothing is scored and nothing is timed, and the ticks only exist so you can stop on Tuesday and pick up on Thursday. The cheat sheet holds the rules that cannot be found by clicking: the ninety day trap, how a split is decided, who can see what. Keep it open when you are on the phone. An owner can see how far each advisor has got on the training page under Admin.

Section 2 of 11

Two boards, and which one somebody goes on

There are two boards and they are not a choice between them. They are the same road, and people move from the first to the second without anybody dragging anything.

  • Leads is people. Somebody rang, a form came in, a client sent a friend. The question it answers is "who have I got and who have I not rung back".
  • Sales opportunities is trips. Every card is a reservation that already exists, and the column it sits in is worked out from the reservation itself.

Somebody enquiring goes on the lead board. Always, and first. At that point there is a person and a conversation and no trip, so there is nothing for the reservation board to hold.

When the conversation becomes a particular trip, a ship and a week and a price to work out, open their card and press Start a reservation. That is the hand-off, and it is the only step you take by hand.

Two things then happen on their own. The reservation appears on the reservation board in the Inquiry column, and the person drops off the lead board and is counted as converted. You never type the word "inquiry" anywhere: that column means a reservation that is still a quote and has not been sent yet, and the moment you send it the card moves to Quote sent by itself.

The same is true of every column after it. Deposit paid, final payment due, paid in full, away now, home: each one is read off the reservation's own dates and payments. A board you have to keep tidy by hand is a board that is wrong by Thursday, so this one cannot be dragged at all.

If you skip the lead board and go straight to a reservation, nothing breaks. You lose the record of the conversation before the trip existed, and the reports that say where clients come from have one less thing to go on.

Section 3 of 11

Taking a reservation

Everything starts on Reservations. A reservation is one trip for one client with one vendor. A family of four on one booking is one reservation with four travellers on it, not four reservations.

One vendor is meant literally. A cruise with air and insurance on it is three reservations, one per vendor, each with its own confirmation number, its own pricing and its own commission. Filing them together reads as tidier and costs you the air commission, because there is one row to chase and it is the cruise's. To give the client one page for all of them, keep them separate and put them together as one trip afterwards (see What the client can see).

The status

  • Quoted is a price you have given that nobody has said yes to.
  • Booked is confirmed with the vendor. This is what counts towards your production.
  • Travelled is set for you once the return date has passed, so you never have to remember to do it.
  • Cancelled keeps the record and takes it out of every total.

Travellers and passports

Start typing a traveller's name and the portal finds anybody already on your books and fills in their passport name, date of birth and document details. It never types over something you have already entered, so if you filled in the passport by hand and then picked the client, the one you typed stands.

The name a vendor matches documents on is the name on the passport, not what you call them. That is why the client record keeps both.

Money owed, and the reminder that sits in front of it

Every final balance is two lines, and this is the one idea in the portal worth understanding properly.

  • The vendor deadline is the real one. Miss it and the booking can be cancelled.
  • The reminder sits ten days in front of it. It is your own buffer, not the vendor's date: a client who has to move money needs longer than a weekend.

They are the same money written down twice, so every total counts the vendor deadline only. If both were counted, a $2,000 balance would read as $4,000. When you post a payment, the reminder in front of it is adjusted or removed to match, so you are never chased for money you have taken.

Posting a payment against the reminder line instead of the deadline is a thing people do, and the portal quietly moves it to the right one rather than recording it somewhere no total looks at.

Where the two lines come from

They do not appear on their own. A reservation has a payment schedule, and until something is on it there is nothing to chase and nothing to remind anybody about. A trip can carry a final payment date, look completely normal in the list, and still have an empty schedule, and in that state the portal will never say a word about it.

Open the reservation and press Build schedule, or use the same button on Payments or on Fill in the gaps. It reads the deposit and the final payment date off the reservation and writes the lines. It never marks anything paid and it never emails anybody.

The one shape worth knowing: inside ninety days of departure there is no deposit, because a cruise is paid in full at booking. The whole trip cost goes in the deposit box and there is no separate balance to chase later. That is correct, and it is why a reservation worth several thousand dollars can have nothing outstanding once the payment is posted.

If a client has already paid, post the payment rather than leaving the schedule empty. An empty schedule and a paid trip look identical to the portal, and only one of them is true.

Part payments

Post what actually arrived. The line becomes the amount received, and the remainder becomes its own line on the same date, so the deadline is not lost with the part that was paid. Nothing in the portal has to learn a new idea: a paid line is paid and a scheduled line is due.

Reminding the client

You can send a payment reminder from the reservation whenever you like. You can also turn on automatic ones in Settings, which send three weeks out, one week out, the day before, and once if the date passes unpaid. They go out over your name, and a reply comes back to you. They are off until you turn them on, because nobody should inherit email to their clients by default. Every one has an unsubscribe link in the footer, and a client who uses it is skipped from then on: see Who has opted out.

Dates

Every date on every screen shows its year, so "Nov 6" cannot be this November or next. When you type a date into a form you do not need to tab across it: once the year is in, the cursor moves to the next date field on its own. Correcting a month or a day does not make it jump, only finishing the year does.

Group space, started from the cruise line's proposal

On Group space, a new group can start from the cruise line's own paperwork. Upload the group proposal or contract as a PDF and the details are filled in for you to check: name, group number, ship, dates, port, cabins, guests, option date, the rep, and the rate grid. Nothing is saved until you press the button at the bottom, so it is safe to try.

  • Margaritaville at Sea, Norwegian and Celebrity are each read in full, from the real layout of their document. A Margaritaville proposal number is kept with its revision, so 3178-2 stays 3178-2 and is not mistaken for 3178.
  • Any other cruise line is read more cautiously: only a detail the document clearly labels is filled in, and it says which ones it left for you. It would rather leave a box empty than put something in it that might be wrong.
  • A category is the room type. Celebrity's A2, C4 and I1 go in the rate grid as room types, with the cabins in each counted and checked against the quote's own totals. If anything disagrees the grid stays empty and the fares go in the notes exactly as printed.
  • What it says it could not find is listed under the upload. A date a contract does not state, such as Norwegian's option date, stays blank for you, and a guest count is only filled in when the payments in the contract prove it.
  • A locked PDF is opened where it can be. A scanned picture of a page has no text to read, and the portal says so instead of guessing.

The group number is the web address people sign up on, so leave it blank if the cruise line has not issued one yet. It can be edited later.

The itinerary, from a confirmation

On a reservation's itinerary, Read a confirmation takes the PDF you were sent (a flight e-ticket, a hotel booking, a tour voucher, a rail ticket, a transfer) and proposes the lines for the trip: a flight with its hour, terminal and seat, a hotel as a check in and a check out, a tour with its start time and where to meet. You see every line, untick the ones that are wrong and press Add to the itinerary. Reading alone saves nothing.

  • It only fills in what the confirmation labels. Anything it cannot place is left out, and what it could not find is said, because a wrong time in front of a client is worse than a missing one.
  • A time printed with no am or pm is read as the 24 hour clock, and the screen says so. Check those first.
  • A date with no year takes the year nearest the trip.
  • It also offers the confirmation number, vendor and dates, but only where the reservation has none of its own. A confirmation that disagrees with the reservation's number is a warning and never an overwrite.
  • Reading the same PDF twice marks the lines already on the itinerary, so nothing is added twice.
  • A scanned picture of a page has no text to read, and a locked PDF may not open; it says so. Lines are added but the client cannot see them until Client sees it is ticked on the itinerary.

No airline's or hotel site's layout has been read exactly yet. It works on what they all label, which is most of it. When one is read wrongly, send the confirmation and it gets fixed for everybody.

Section 4 of 11

Commission, and who keeps what

Commission is what the vendor pays. You can type it, or record a percentage and let the portal work it out from the trip cost.

The number beside the money

Where a figure has a second, smaller number next to it, that is your share. The larger one is the commission on the booking; the smaller one is what you keep after the agency's share.

How the share is decided

Three answers, in order: a percentage written on that reservation, then your standing agreement, then all of it.

Your agreement can have two rates: one for business you generate and a lower one for leads the agency hands you. A reservation marked as a company lead uses the second.

Two things are yours whatever the split says: a tour conductor credit and a vendor bonus. Those are earned by filling a group or hitting a target rather than by the agency's terms with the vendor. Casino rates are the exception and are split like anything else, because those run on the agency's licence.

Your own travel

A reservation set to My own travel rather than Client reservation is not split. Booking your own holiday through the agency earns real commission and the agency takes none of it, whatever your agreement says and whatever figure is on the trip. It is also left out of production totals by default, since it is not client business.

No commission

Marking a reservation no commission means nobody earns: not you and not the agency. It is not zero-for-you and full-for-them. Those reservations still count as trips and as sales, and they are left out of every commission total, including Fill in the gaps, which will not ask you for a figure that would contradict the status you set.

Getting paid

Commission owed is what vendors have not yet paid. A reservation is pending until the money is in and received once it is, and that is the whole list: there is no step in the middle for having sent the invoice, because it was a claim about paperwork that nobody kept up to date.

Four figures, on the page and on the trip itself. Expected is what the vendor owes. Received is what has actually arrived. Taken is the agency's share of what arrived and Paid out is yours, which is what you are paid and is not the same number as your share of what is expected until the vendor has settled in full. Where no split has been agreed the agency takes nothing and all of it is yours, and the page says so rather than leaving you to notice.

Being paid

Two different things, and the page keeps them apart. Paid out is what the agency has actually handed you. Still to pay is your share of money that has arrived and not yet reached you, which is what the next payout run is about.

The agency records a payout against the reservations it covers, so a trip stops appearing on the run once it has been paid. If the vendor sends more later, a bonus in June against a base settled in March, that part becomes owed again on its own without anybody having to notice.

Only the agency records one. An advisor who could would be declaring themselves paid.

Record what arrives rather than ticking a box. Enter the amount the vendor sent, and if they pay short the difference stays on the page as owed, which is how you find the one booking they quietly underpaid. Marking a reservation received in a batch records the outstanding amount for you, so the label and the money cannot disagree. A vendor statement can be filed and reconciled against what the portal expected.

Section 5 of 11

Clients and prospects

A client is somebody who has booked. A prospect is somebody carried over from the old CRM who has not. Both live on Clients, with a filter at the top that defaults to clients.

They share one page on purpose. If prospects were hidden somewhere else you would type a name the portal already knows and create a second copy of the same person. So even when the filter says Clients, a search that matches a prospect says so and offers to show them.

The record

Everything a vendor asks for at the worst possible moment: passport name, number, issuing country and expiry, date of birth, address, known traveler and redress numbers, and past-guest numbers for each cruise line. The passport expiry shows a warning when there is less than six months left, which is what most cruise lines require at the return date.

Two fields worth filling in that are easy to skip. Goes by is what to call them, and emails use it. Where they came from is the only field in the portal that answers which of your marketing is working.

Households

Several clients at one address, kept together so you can see what the house is worth and stop sending two of everything. You can build one from the Clients list or from anybody's own record.

Possibly the same person

Above the client list, a box offers pairs that might be one person typed twice: the same email, the same phone number or the same name. A shared number is often a family, so there are three answers. Keep one when it really is the same person: everything on the other moves across and nothing is lost. Same household when they are family, such as a husband and wife with one number or a child using a parent's email: both stay as clients, kept together. Different people when they are unrelated: both stay and the pair is not offered again, and you can undo it straight away. Two people already in one household are never offered.

Client credits

Future cruise credits and vendor credits with an expiry, so the one somebody is holding turns up on Who to call before it runs out rather than after.

Importing

Import clients takes a spreadsheet or a CSV, including a Cruise Planners client export as it comes. It shows you what it will do before it does it. Import reservations does the same for a back-office export.

Whose clients these are

Your clients, reservations, leads and the trips under a supplier are yours. Another advisor cannot see them, open them or find them in a search, and you cannot see theirs. The supplier directory itself, the contacts and the rates, is the agency's, so everybody sees that. Whoever runs the agency sees everything, and on screens that have an All advisors picker, such as leads, marketing and the tiles across the top of a page, can narrow it to one person.

Section 6 of 11

What the client can see

Pages you can send them, and another way in they can use on their own. The pages are public web addresses with no password, switched on by you and off by you. None of them carries a word about commission.

One trip

Every reservation can produce a page of its own from The client's own page on the record. It carries the itinerary day by day, who is travelling, what the trip costs with the payment schedule and what is still outstanding, any documents you have shared, the choices you offered with a way for them to pick one, and a box to send you a question that arrives in the portal, with your answers appearing in the same thread. It installs on a phone like an app. You can see whether they have opened it and how often.

Everything you hold for them

Their own page on a client record does the same thing one level up: one link showing that person every trip they have with you. Bookings whose dates overlap, give or take a couple of days, are drawn as one holiday with its own total, what is paid and what is still to pay, and each booking listed underneath opening its own page. A trip you have put together yourself, described below, is always drawn as one holiday whatever its dates, and links to its own itinerary page. Quotes you have sent sit in their own block, one row each, because a quote is a question and two questions are not one holiday. Then where they have been, and how to reach you.

A family in two cabins. Quote it as one reservation and book it as two. While they are deciding they want one page with one total, so put both cabins on a single reservation as pricing lines and name them: "Balcony 8102, Mum and Dad" and "Interior 6210, the boys". Once they say yes the cruise line gives you two confirmation numbers, two deposits and two final payment dates, and a reservation holds one of each, so split it then. Nothing is lost by splitting: the dates match, so their own page draws the two back together as one holiday with a combined total, while each cabin keeps the payment schedule the vendor actually set. The date you have to watch is the final payment one, and it is the reason not to leave two cabins on one record.

Turning it on also gives each booked trip its own page, so every line on the list opens. It never touches a quote. A quote appears only once you have sent it, because sending it is what says the client may see it, and one button on a client record must not publish a price you were still deciding.

A trip opened from that list carries a link back to it. The same trip opened from its own link does not, and that is deliberate: you might forward one trip to somebody travelling with them, and a page reachable from it that lists everything that client has ever booked would be a different thing entirely from what you meant to send.

One holiday, several reservations

A holiday is often several reservations: the flights, three hotels, a train, a few tours. Each has its own page, and a client should not have to open nine of them. On Reservations, tick the ones that belong together and press Put together as one trip. Give it a name, and you land on the trip's own screen.

The page a client gets has the whole itinerary in date order: a hotel appears on the day it starts and the day it ends, flights on each way, and any lines you have added to a reservation's itinerary (and marked ready to share) land on the right day, with times. Days with nothing on them say so. Below that, every booking on the trip opens its own page for documents, payments and messages, then your welcome at the top and a Good to know box at the bottom for tips and who to call, both written on the trip's screen. The costs are added up across the reservations, and left off a printed copy.

Only booked or travelled reservations go in; a quote does not, because it would put a price on a page nobody had agreed to. A reservation is in one trip at a time, and a cancelled one drops off the page by itself. You can add and remove reservations later, turn the link off, or take the trip apart: the reservations themselves are never changed. A trip you have put together is always drawn as one holiday on the client's own page, whatever its dates.

Signing in, instead of holding a link

A client can also go to /portal on this site, type the email address you have on their record, and be sent a link that signs them in. It works once, it lasts twenty minutes, and there is no password for them to forget or for you to reset on a Saturday. What they see is their own page: every trip, what is owed and when, their documents, and a way to send you a note.

Two things this does that a link cannot. It knows who they are, so somebody who is on the books of two advisors in this agency sees everything the agency holds for them in one place rather than in two links. And it can be withdrawn from one person without withdrawing it from the rest of the household, because the address is the identity rather than the link.

It only works if the address they type is the address on the record. That is the whole of the check, so a client record with a typo in the email is a client who cannot sign in, and one with no email at all is a client who can only be sent a link.

Turning one off

Off is permanent for that link. The code is dropped rather than hidden, so switching the page back on mints a new address and the old one stays dead. That is on purpose: a link that stops working and then starts working again months later, pointing at trips that have moved on, is worse than a link that is gone. Turning a client's page off leaves their individual trip pages working; those are switched off one trip at a time.

What is never on either

Commission, in any form. The split, what the agency takes, what you are paid, the discussion on the record, your notes, and any other client. Treat the links themselves the same way: the address is the whole of the permission, so a client link forwarded on reaches everything you hold for that person.

Section 7 of 11

The day's work

  • To do: tasks, due dates and reminders, including ones that repeat and templates for the set of things every new booking needs. Each morning you get one email listing what is due and what is late, and only one: it is sent once a day however many times the portal looks.
  • Who to call: the calls nothing else is chasing you about. Credits about to expire, passports about to, clients home from a trip, birthdays and anniversaries coming round, and people who have travelled with you and have nothing booked.
  • Fill in the gaps: reservations missing a cost, a commission figure or a vendor deadline. A trip with no deadline cannot be chased and a trip with no cost is missing from every total, so this is the shortest list worth clearing.
  • Notes a client leaves on their own trip page arrive by email and sit on the reservation. Answer from the reservation page: your reply appears under their note on their trip page, as one conversation, and is emailed to them so they do not have to go looking. This is separate from Chat, which clients never see.

Section 8 of 11

Talking to each other

Chat is for the people who work here. There is no way for a client to reach it, see it, or be added to it, and that is the point rather than an oversight.

Three places a conversation happens

  • Rooms are open to everybody in the agency. Anyone can make one.
  • Direct messages are between two of you.
  • A discussion on a reservation or a client sits on that record, under everything else, marked "Only the agency sees this".

The third one is the reason this exists. "The deposit is wobbling", "the rep at this line never answers", "I think I quoted this wrong" are the sentences that currently live in somebody's text messages and are gone by the time another advisor picks the file up. Written on the booking, they are still there in March.

Asking somebody in particular

Type @ and a list of people appears: keep typing to narrow it, then press Enter or Tab, or click a name, to choose one. They get it on the bell, as a notification on whatever screen they are on, on their phone if they have turned that on, and in the next morning's email if they have not read it by then. @here asks everybody who is in that room.

If two people share a first name it will not guess between them, so nobody is named at all. Type both names and it will find the right one.

Being told

Somebody writing to you is worth hearing about when it happens, and there are three layers to that.

  • The portal is open and in front: a card appears on whatever screen you are on, including the chat page when the message is in a different room from the one you are reading, with who wrote and the start of it. Click it to open the conversation. It only announces what arrives while you are there; a backlog is the number beside Chat.
  • You are somewhere else: if you have turned on notifications on this computer or phone (in Settings), the message knocks on it straight away and the notification shows who wrote. It carries nothing private through Google or Apple: your device asks the portal what is waiting.
  • You have not read it by morning: it is in the next email.

A muted conversation does none of these. A discussion on a reservation or client only appears in your list once somebody has written in it, so the list shows conversations rather than every record you have ever opened.

Who can read what

Rooms and the discussions on records belong to the agency. Whoever runs it can read them, including after somebody leaves, the same as every reservation and every client note.

Direct messages do not. Nobody else can open one, and that includes an owner working inside your account: while they are doing that, your direct messages are not on the screen at all and cannot be reached.

Worth knowing rather than discovering: anything typed here about a client's money is a record, the same as a note on their file.

When it tells you

A message you have not read shows as a count beside Chat. The card and the notification on your phone or computer are described above under Being told. Being named by name also puts a line on the bell and in the morning email. All of it is switchable in Settings, one switch at a time. Turning the card off does not turn the count off: the count is a number on a screen you are already looking at, and it does not stop being true because you looked.

Nobody sits in this all day. There are no typing dots and nothing expects a fast reply.

Retiring a room

Whoever made a room can close it, and so can the agency. Closing is not deleting: everything said in it is kept and readable, it stops taking new messages, and it moves to Closed at the bottom of the list where it can be opened again. A direct message is not a room and cannot be closed; a discussion belongs to the record it is on.

Section 9 of 11

Finding the next booking

Sales opportunities

Sales opportunities is every live reservation by stage, so you can see what is sitting at quoted and needs a nudge. You see your own; an agency owner can pick one advisor or all of them.

Lists

Lists answers "who should I write to" from what people have actually booked, rather than from a tag somebody applied once. Sailing in the next sixty days, home in the last thirty, nothing booked, quoted and never booked, sailed with a particular line, a credit about to expire, a passport about to, a birthday or anniversary coming up, came from a particular source, lives in a particular state.

Tick the rules and the count and a sample update as you type. A saved list is saved as the rules, not as the people, so "sailing in the next sixty days" still means that in November.

Emails

Emails sends one message to one list, once. Press Write to them on a saved list and you arrive with it chosen.

  • {{first_name}} uses what they go by where you have recorded one, and {{name}} is their full name. The preview renders against a real client on the list and names any field that comes back blank, so you find out before you send rather than afterwards.
  • It goes out under your name, and a reply comes back to you.
  • Send yourself a test first. It carries the same From and reply-to a real one does.
  • The confirmation says the number out loud. "Send to 412 people?" is a different question from "Send?"
  • The people are written down when you press send, so the record of who got it cannot change afterwards. It goes out in batches over the following minutes, about three hundred an hour, and you can close the page or stop it part way.

Who has opted out

Every email to a client carries an unsubscribe link in its footer: a quote, an invoice, a payment reminder, a form, a reply, the link they sign in with, as well as the emails sent to a list. There is no switch to turn that off. Somebody who opts out is dropped as the message goes out rather than when the list was built, so an opt-out during a send is still honoured.

The opt-out list is the agency's, so everybody sees the same one. You can add somebody who asked you on the phone, and you can put somebody back who unsubscribed by accident and then rang up about it. It shows on their client record too, so you know before you write to them personally.

What unsubscribing stops is the automatic email. Offers and news, automations, and the payment reminders the portal sends on its own are not sent to somebody who has opted out. Something you send by hand, such as a quote or an invoice somebody asked for, still goes. That means a client who opts out stops getting automatic payment reminders: the dates still apply, so ring them. The client's record says they have opted out.

Specials, forms and automations

  • Specials: a deal with its own public page and a short code you can post or email. Enquiries come back into the portal against that deal.
  • Forms: a hosted form on its own address. A submission becomes a client record on your own book, marked as a prospect until they book.
  • Automations: when something happens, do something. A form submitted, a reservation made, a final payment coming up, somebody home from a trip, a client choosing an option on a quote. The action can be an email, a task, a note, a tag, or handing the contact to a campaign you have already built in CTT Tools.

Section 11 of 11

Things that catch people out

"It says past due and I have paid it"

Check which of the two lines you posted against. The reminder sits ten days in front of the vendor deadline and is the same money; posting against the reminder is moved to the deadline for you, but a reminder left over from an older schedule can still look like a balance. Opening the reservation shows both.

"Nothing is reminding me about a trip"

Its payment schedule is probably empty. A final payment date on its own warns nobody: the reminders are built from the schedule, and a reservation with no lines on it has nothing to remind you about. Press Build schedule on the reservation, or on Fill in the gaps, which lists every trip in that state with the amount that is on no line.

"The commission total does not match what I added up"

Most likely one of three things: a no-commission reservation, which is excluded on purpose; a cancelled one, which is excluded from every total; or you are comparing the commission on the booking with your share of it.

"I cannot find a client I know is in there"

The Clients page defaults to people who have booked. Search finds prospects too and says so, or switch the filter at the top to Everyone.

"Nothing is being emailed"

This is almost always the agency's mail setup rather than anything you did, and it is not something you can see or fix from here. Ask your agency owner to check the admin page. Nothing is lost while it is sorted out: a send with no mail key waits rather than marking everybody as failed, so it goes out once the setup is right.

You run this agency, so there is a second half to this manual: running the agency covers advisors, splits, the agency record and how to tell whether the portal's scheduled work is still running.

Something here wrong, or missing? Tell your agency owner and it gets fixed in the portal rather than in a document nobody reads.

Running the agency

The parts of the portal only an owner can see.

The advisor manual

Everything an advisor needs is in their own manual, and you see that portal too. This page is the other half: the settings that decide what every advisor can do, what a client sees with the agency's name on it, and how to tell whether the portal is still doing its work when nobody is watching.

The admin pages

The ones you will use most are in the menu on the left: Advisors, the agency record, Trainings and events and Training, which shows how far each advisor has got through the getting started course and who has stopped.

Corporate targets

Under Report, Corporate targets is the agency's own targets for the year, and only an owner sees the line or can open it. Set what the agency means to book, the commission it earns, what it keeps itself after its advisors' shares, and how many reservations. Each is shown against where the agency should be by today, with who the figures are made of beside it. Count them by when reservations were taken or by when people travel, and say whether advisors' own travel counts. An advisor's own Targets are separate and stay theirs.

Who may sell into a group

An advisor sets each of their groups to Private, Open to the agency or By request, and you see which on Group pages and on every group, with who changed it last and when. You can filter that list by setting.

  • You see and book into everything. Private only hides a group from other advisors, never from you.
  • The commission for outside bookings is yours alone. On a group, Commission an outside advisor keeps is the share a reservation from another advisor keeps. Left empty, they follow their own agreement. It is copied onto the reservation when it is booked, so changing it later does not touch what is already in the group, and taking a reservation out of the group takes the share off again. A share you set by hand on a reservation is never overwritten.

Trainings and events

A vendor training, a webinar or an office meeting goes on Trainings and events once and lands on every advisor's calendar and in the Trainings and events panel on their dashboard. Give it a name, a day, a start time and the time zone the invitation states, and paste the join link; the link becomes a Join button. You can also press Add an event in the Trainings and events panel on your own dashboard.

  • Who is invited. The default is every advisor. Choose Choose which advisors to pick some by name: only they see it on their calendar and dashboard, and only they are emailed. You always see every event. Changing the time never changes who is invited.
  • When somebody is going. Each time an advisor presses I'm going (or takes it back) you are emailed the name, the day and time in your own zone, and the headcount so far: "5 of 12 invited advisors are going". Pressing it twice sends nothing more.
  • Opening an event. Clicking a training on the calendar, or its title on the dashboard, opens its details: when, who is giving it, the notes, a Join the call button, who is going, and buttons to add it to Google Calendar or Outlook. If there is no join link yet it says so: add one with Edit on this page.
  • Time zones. Enter the event once, in the zone its invitation states. Each advisor reads it in their own: a 2:00 PM Eastern webinar is 1:00 PM to someone on Central time, on their calendar, their dashboard and in their email. An advisor sets their zone in Settings, and you can set it for them from Edit on the advisors list. Until it is set, Eastern is used. Times are shown with AM and PM everywhere in the portal.
  • Tell them. Leave Email every advisor about it when I save ticked and each invited advisor gets an email with the day, the time, the join link, your notes, buttons to add it to Google Calendar or Outlook, and a file for Apple Calendar. Later, Email an update sends the change, and cancelling offers to tell them it is off. The list shows when they were last emailed.
  • A reminder the day before. Advisors who have not ticked I'm going get one note, in the afternoon, the day before. Untick the reminder on an event to turn it off for that one. An event you only just emailed about is not reminded on top of it.
  • Advisors only read it. The one thing they can do is tick I'm going. You see who ticked on the same page, as a count out of your advisors and their names, which is the headcount a vendor asks for.
  • Cancel keeps it, Delete removes it. A cancelled event stays on the calendar struck through, so nobody turns up to a training that is off. Delete takes it off every calendar and clears the answers.
  • An appointment you make for yourself on the calendar is still yours alone. Only this page puts something on everybody's.

Travel insurance waiver wording

Every quote and reservation carries a link where the client declines travel insurance in writing. Waiver wording is where you write what they read: why insurance is recommended, the line they tick, and the warning under the form. Each box is separate and one you clear goes back to the built-in text, so you can change a single sentence. Write {agency} and {advisor} where the names should go; each client sees the name of their own advisor. The page shows how it will read. A waiver a client has already signed keeps the words they saw, so changing the wording never alters a signed one. Have whoever you rely on for legal advice read it before you use your own text, since it is your agency's waiver.

Client emails

Once you switch them on, every reservation that is booked from then on sends its client a run of emails by itself, in the advisor's name, with nothing for the advisor to do: a note that the trip is booked, a reminder a week and two days before the deposit and before the final payment (only while it is unpaid), a nudge to check passports two months out, online check-in two weeks out, bon voyage three days out, and a welcome home the day after the trip ends. Client emails has the switch and the words.

  • New reservations only. Switching them on never writes to anyone you already have. Only reservations made after you press the button are planned.
  • Only trips. A cruise, hotel, resort, package, tour or rail booking gets them. A travel insurance policy, a transfer, an excursion, a ticket or a flight on its own never does.
  • The words are the agency's. Open any email, change the subject or the message, see how it reads, and press Email me a test to get a copy yourself. Put the client's own details in with curly brackets, like {first_name}, {trip} and {phone}; a line that mentions something a reservation does not have, like a confirmation number nobody has typed, is left out. A couple is greeted by both names.
  • Notes advisors send themselves use the same page. The first note to a new lead, the follow-up note, and the notes from Who to call for a client who has gone quiet, a birthday and an anniversary are all here too. They only go when an advisor reads one and presses send.
  • Advisors stay in charge. On a reservation the card Emails going out on this trip shows every email and the day it goes. An advisor can read exactly what a client will get with See it, skip one, or switch them all off for a trip the client already knows about.
  • You can see what went. Recently sent at the bottom of the page lists the last 25: when, which email, which client, which advisor, and the reason for any that could not be sent.
  • Advisors are copied. By default each advisor gets a silent copy of every email their clients are sent, so they see exactly what went in their name. There is a tick for it on the Client emails page, and it is worth keeping on for the first few weeks.
  • Read them first. Tick Let me read each email before it goes and each one waits on the Client emails page, under Waiting for your OK, with buttons to read it, send it or skip it. Your dashboard says when some are waiting. An email you do not get to for three days is dropped like any late one. Untick it once you trust the wording and they go out on their own again.
  • Safe by design. An email more than three days late is dropped, never sent late. A client who unsubscribes is not sent another. An advisor who already uses the automatic payment reminders does not get a second set of payment emails.

Confirmations that make themselves

An advisor who forwards a vendor confirmation to their own address gets the reservation made for them, when it reads cleanly and came from their own email address and the mail system confirmed that. Everything else is held for them with the reason, and they are emailed either way. The Import page also says, for anything pasted or uploaded, whether an email like it would have been made automatically. If an advisor reports that theirs are always held with the reason "the mail system did not confirm who sent it", tell whoever maintains the portal: the activity log records what the message actually carried.

Leads waiting on an answer

Leads from the website forms land on each advisor's Leads board. A notice on the dashboard and a banner on the page say how many have not heard back, and Answer them now walks through them one at a time with a note already written. The first note and the follow-up are the agency's wording, edited under Client emails above. Nothing is sent until an advisor reads it and presses send.

Tax and 1099

Tax and 1099 in the admin menu is where you keep what you need on each advisor to report what you paid them at the end of the year. For each one you record their legal name, the kind of taxpayer they are, the last four digits of their tax number, a mailing address and the date their W-9 arrived. Only the last four digits are ever kept: the whole number stays on the signed W-9 in your own records.

  • What it shows. What each advisor was paid in the year you choose, whether that is $600 or more (so a 1099-NEC is due), and who has no W-9 on file.
  • A reminder. If you have paid an advisor this year and hold no W-9 for them, your dashboard says so.
  • January. Download the 1099 file gives a spreadsheet with a row for each advisor you paid, in the order the form is filled in. It is for you or your tax preparer to file from, not a filed form. Anyone who lives outside the country is marked, and filed differently.

Advisors

  • Nobody signs themselves up. Create the account and send the link it gives you, which lets them choose their own password. You never see or set that password, which is the point of it.
  • Suspending is how an account is ended. It stops a stale login working immediately. Deleting is not offered, because their reservations, commission and client records are the agency's and have to stay readable after they have gone.
  • Time zone. Edit on an advisor's row has a Time zone field. Trainings and events, and the emails about them, are shown in it, and an advisor can set their own in Settings. Until it is set, Eastern is used.
  • Working as an advisor. You can stand in an advisor's shoes to see exactly what they see, which is the fastest way to answer "it does not show me that". It is offered on an advisor's record and never on your own. While it is on, a banner runs across every screen: the one way this goes wrong is forgetting, and filing a reservation under the wrong advisor.

Splits, and why they are only here

The standing agreement, and the lower rate for company-supplied leads, are set on the advisor's record and only there. An advisor cannot set their own share by any route: not on a reservation, not on an import, not through the API. That was true the hard way, and the three ways round it are closed and stay closed.

A change to the agreement reaches new work only. Every reservation is stamped with the agreement the moment it is taken, so what a trip sold in March earns is what was agreed in March, and a report run in September agrees with the cheque. Change a rate today and nothing already on the books moves.

This is worth knowing because it used to be the other way round, and the old behaviour is the one people expect: the figure was worked out live from the advisor's record, so lowering a split restated every past trip that had no rate of its own, including ones already invoiced and already paid out. If you remember being told to write the old rate onto past reservations before changing an agreement, that is no longer necessary and has not been since the whole book was frozen at the rates it then read.

Two things still beat the stamp, in this order. A rate written on one reservation wins over everything, which is how you handle a one-off. Then the stamp itself. Only where a trip carries neither does it fall through to the advisor's record, and after the freeze no trip does.

An advisor with no rate recorded keeps everything. The chain ends at 100 per cent rather than at nothing, on the grounds that no agreement is not an agreement to work for free. So a new advisor booking before their split is written down is a real problem with a silent symptom: their trips read as 100 per cent and the agency's share of them reads $0.00. Record the rate when the account is made, not when the first commission arrives.

The payout run

The one thing on the money screens only you can do. It is on Commission owed in the ordinary portal rather than in here, because it is read alongside everything else about the money; the button appears for you and for nobody else. It is also hidden while you are working as an advisor, since a payout recorded in somebody else's shoes would be that advisor declaring themselves paid.

Record a payout asks four things: who, the date, how it went, and a reference such as a check number. It does not ask an amount. It records everything currently owed to that advisor out of money the vendors have actually sent, which is the figure the page is already showing, and the reservations it covers drop off the run.

That last part is the reason the run is worth trusting. It lists what is owed rather than what has been earned, so a trip whose commission has not arrived from the vendor is not on it. And if more arrives later, a bonus in June against a base settled in March, that part becomes owed again on its own without anybody having to spot it.

Two sanity checks before you send money. Received should reconcile with what the vendors actually paid, because everything below it is a share of that figure and nothing else. And an advisor showing a suspiciously round 100 per cent is usually one whose split was never recorded rather than one who negotiated well.

Advisors never see the agency's side. What the agency keeps of a split is shown to owners only. For an advisor it is left out of what the portal sends, so it is not on the dashboard, the commissions page, the reservation or the reports, and it cannot be read off the network either. They see what they keep and what they are paid, and the full commission on their own trips, which they have always been told.

The agency record

Name, address, phone, seller-of-travel registration, logo and colours. These are not decoration: they are what a client sees on a quote, a statement, a deal page and a group page, and the registration number is there because in several states it has to be.

The brand colour is checked for contrast before it is used on white. A colour too pale to read is kept for the places it works and swapped for a readable one where it would be text, rather than shipping a page the client has to squint at.

The agency record also holds the card authorization form address, the secure form your clients fill in to give a card for a deposit or payment. Once it is set (paste the full https:// address in Agencies), it appears in a quote that asks for a deposit, in an invoice, in every payment reminder, on the client's trip page beside what is still owed, and as a Card form button on the reservation. The portal never takes the card itself.

Commissions: checks in, advisors paid

Under Commissions in this menu. Four figures across the top, and the one you press decides the list underneath: Received in the year (with what the fees took, so you can see what reached the bank), Paid out to advisors, Scheduled to pay out, and Still owed to us by vendors. The year at the top changes the first two.

Recording a check

Press Record a check. If the vendor sent a remittance PDF, drop it in: it fills in the check number, the amount and a line for each traveller, and finds the reservation for each one by its reference or the traveller's name. It only offers a reservation when it is sure, and a surname on its own is a short list for you to choose from, not an answer. For anything it cannot read, type it in. The Travel Leaders Network remittance PDF is read directly, and a commission statement PDF from any other vendor is read as rows. You can also drop in a screenshot of a statement, or paste one with Ctrl or Cmd and V while the form is open: the portal's AI copies the rows off it into the paste box, where you can correct them before they are read, and then it carries on exactly as pasted rows do. A picture is only as good as the screenshot, so a clear one of just the table works best.

Any other vendor. Open Paste rows from any vendor's statement in the same box, or choose a CSV file saved from the vendor's spreadsheet. One reservation on each row, with its confirmation number and what was paid; a heading row that says which column is the commission or amount, the guest and the confirmation number helps and is not needed. Each row is matched to a reservation by its confirmation number first and the guest's name second, you check every line, and nothing is recorded until you press Save the check. A pasted statement has no check total, so the total starts as the sum of the lines: change it to the amount on the check.

Each line has a Pay out date, a choice of the next 1sts and 15ths. It suggests the first pay date on or after 30 days before sailing, so a cruise leaving March 3 goes out on February 1, and one that was paid for inside those 30 days goes out on the next pay date. That is the point at which a cruise line can no longer take the commission back if the client cancels. Change it if you want. You can record a check before every line is matched and add the rest later.

Is the check at the bank?

A check you record starts as not at the bank yet. When the money has landed, open the check and press It is at the bank; press the same button again, which then says Deposited with the date, to undo it. Until you do, an advisor who is owed money from that check shows a check not at the bank yet badge on Scheduled to pay out, and Mark paid warns you. It only warns: it never stops you paying. Checks recorded before this existed count as deposited on the day they arrived, and money typed in on a single reservation, with no check behind it, is never flagged.

Reports

The Reports tile has ten reports, each a table you can download: By advisor, By vendor, By month sailed, By month received, Short and over, Every check, Vendor pay speed, Paid to advisors (1099), Fees & expenses and Year-end summary. The first three share the same columns: reservations, expected, received, the advisor's share of what was received, paid out, still due to the advisor, and what the agency keeps, with a Total row. Sails from and Sails to narrow the reports built from reservations by the day each one sails; the ones built from checks list every check in the year chosen at the top. Vendor pay speed shows how many days after sailing each vendor paid, on average, at the fastest and at the slowest (a negative number means they paid before the sail date). Paid to advisors (1099) totals what each advisor was actually paid in the year, and advisors paid $600 or more usually need a 1099-NEC. Fees & expenses lists what vendors took out of checks, which are agency expenses and never come out of an advisor's share. Year-end summary is one page for your accountant: what came in, the fees, what was deposited, the advisors' share, what was paid and what is still owed, and what the agency kept before and after fees.

When a vendor does not pay

On Still owed to us, each unpaid line has two buttons. Dispute means you have asked the vendor and are waiting on an answer: it moves to its own Disputed group so it is not chased as routine, and it still counts as owed. Write off means you will not collect it: it stops counting as owed, and it is kept under Written off with the reason you gave. Either one asks for a sentence on why. Resolved and Put back undo them. Nothing about what was expected, or what arrived, changes, and an advisor is only ever paid on what actually arrived, so none of this moves an advisor's pay.

Fees

Enter the check total, the amount on the check or remittance, then any fee taken out of it and what the fee was. Each line's Commission Paid Amount is how much of that total belongs to one reservation. The fee is the agency's. It is kept on the check so you can count it as an expense, and it never reduces what an advisor is paid, because their share is a share of what the vendor said they paid. If a check is lower than expected because the commission was entered wrong on the reservation, correct the reservation: the advisor is paid on what actually arrived.

What an advisor sees

Each advisor sees commission that has arrived and is assigned to the next pay date, and nothing scheduled for a later one. Scheduled to pay out shows what each pay date will pay each advisor, and the next one is marked. When you have paid someone, press Mark paid on their line and enter the check number or reference: that records the payment, it does not send money. A payout only takes money assigned to that pay date or earlier.

Correcting something already recorded. A check, with every line on it, is corrected with Edit the check on Received: change the amounts (a minus is money taken back), the pay days, the check total or fee, add lines or take them off. A line that was recorded on its own, under Recorded one reservation at a time (or opened from All commissions), has its own Edit: the amount, the day received, the reference, the pay day, or Remove this line. A payout is corrected with Edit under Paid out (the day, how and the reference); to change its amount or what it covers, Undo it and record it again. Once an advisor has been paid on a reservation, its amounts cannot go down and its lines cannot be removed until that payout is taken back.

Not scheduled yet. On Record a check, the pay out date for a line can be Not scheduled yet: the money is recorded as received, but no pay day gets it and advisors are not shown it. It appears on Scheduled to pay out in its own group, "No pay date yet", marked Not scheduled, and is counted in the Scheduled total like the other site. Press Edit there, or change the date on the check's line, to give it a real pay day.

Still owed to us is laid out like the other portal: search a client, confirmation number or vendor, and Download what I see. Beside each reservation, Record opens Record a check with that reservation already on the first line (enter the check total, and change the amount to what the vendor actually paid), and Won’t be paid takes it out of what is owed, with a sentence on why. Dispute sets it apart while you wait on the vendor. The same buttons are on every row of All commissions, where a written off reservation shows Put back.

The vendor shorted you, or took money back? Add a line for that reservation with a minus amount, such as -4.00 (brackets work too). The lines then add up to the check as it came, a +14.00 and a -4.00 on a 10.00 check, and that reservation is shown as paid short or reduced by the amount. It also lowers what the advisor is owed on it.

Already paid one reservation? Open All commissions, find the reservation and press Mark paid beside its Still due figure. Enter the day you paid it and how, and it is recorded as paid to that advisor. It counts the money already received for that one reservation, whatever pay date it was assigned to. If you paid it by mistake, Undo the payout under the Paid out tile.

Only owners record any of this. An advisor cannot enter a receipt, mark a trip received or take a payment off a trip, and the screens do not offer it to them. That is deliberate: someone who could record their own commission could put it on the next payout.

A check cannot be deleted once any of its reservations has been paid out to an advisor, since there is no telling which part paid what. Undo the payout first, then remove the check.

The weekly supplier specials

Every advisor can search Supplier specials under Marketing, and that page is for reading and searching only. You keep it under Supplier specials in this Admin menu: it has the upload, Add one, every offer with an Edit button, and the history of uploads. When the weekly document arrives, press Upload the weekly list there and give it the Word file (.docx). It reads the tables, leaves out the recap of what is ending soon because that repeats the offers above it, and shows you everything it found before anything is added.

Each row is marked new, changed or here. An offer that comes round again next week is updated, not doubled, so a promotion that runs for a month is one entry with the latest dates. Rows whose deadline could not be read say so, and you can correct them with Edit once they are in. Advisors cannot add, change or remove anything.

The same Upload the weekly list button also takes the Travel Leaders Network specials file (a .json). Those are the consumer promotions, and each one keeps its headline, travel period and its See details link exactly as the file gave it, agent id included, so a client who follows it is credited to the agency. An advisor can open the link or press Copy link to send it. A promotion is recognised by the number in its link, so next week's copy updates it and does not add a second card. "Today" in a travel period is replaced with the day the file was pulled, because it stops being true a week later.

Uploaded the wrong file? Undo this upload at the bottom of the page removes the offers it added. Changes it made to offers that were already there stay.

Automations, and the check-in reminder

Under Marketing, Automations. Each one is a trigger and some steps. There is a new trigger, a trip a set number of days from sailing, and the list starts with one built on it: Online check-in reminder. It was added switched off, so nothing has gone to a client. Open it, read the email, and tick On when you are happy.

Reading an email before it goes. On any Send an email step, press See how it reads. It shows the email in the real layout, with the real footer and unsubscribe link, filled in with a sample person (Sam Sample) and sample trip details. A placeholder that depends on what somebody typed in a form, such as one of your own questions, shows in [brackets] and is listed under the preview, so you can see exactly where it will be filled in. Nothing is sent, and the portal does not keep a copy of an email once it has gone: the activity panel (the number under In flight) lists who is on which step and what was done.

  • It runs once for each booked trip, when the trip comes inside 45 days of sailing, and goes to the lead guest's email on their client record. Change the number if the cruise lines you sell open check-in at a different distance, or make a second one for a few days out.
  • A trip that was already inside the line when the automation was switched on is left alone unless it crossed in the last week, so switching it on does not email everybody at once.
  • Trips marked as your own travel and trips that are only quotes are skipped, and anybody who has unsubscribed is skipped.
  • The email carries an unsubscribe link and the reply goes to the advisor who took the booking. That applies to every automation email now: until this change a reply went to the no reply address and was lost.
  • Every automation has an On switch. Off keeps it and stops it, where before the only way to stop one was to delete it.

The privacy policy

A plain language policy is public at /privacy, linked from the sign in page and from every page a client sees. Three things in it are yours to keep true. The contact address is your notification email. It says data is deleted or made anonymous once it is no longer needed, and there is no automatic clean up, so that is done by hand when somebody asks. It also says there is no tracking, and it now says the portal records the date and time a client opens a trip page and which link they followed, and nothing else about them. If any of that changes, change the policy with it, and have somebody qualified read it.

Forwarding email to the portal

Advisors can forward a vendor confirmation to a private address and review it on Import reservations, and forward meeting invites to a second one for the calendar. Both use one address each per advisor, made on request, on a domain you point at the portal. Until that is done the pages say forwarding is not set up, and nothing is lost: pasting the text works the same way.

On this portal it is set up. It runs on cttinbox.com, a domain of its own, and not on cttagents.com. This agency's ordinary mail is Microsoft 365, and Cloudflare Email Routing takes over the mail of whatever domain it is switched on for, so switching it on for cttagents.com would have stopped every advisor's mailbox receiving. Everything arriving at cttinbox.com goes to this portal, which accepts an address only if it carries an advisor's token and refuses the rest with the reason.

The steps below are for setting it up on another domain.

  1. Choose a subdomain that carries no other mail, for example inbox.cttagents.com.
  2. In Cloudflare, open the domain, then Email, then Email Routing. Turn it on and add the subdomain. Cloudflare adds the records it needs.
  3. Create a catch-all rule on that subdomain with the action Send to a Worker, and choose this portal's Worker.
  4. Give the subdomain to whoever maintains the portal, to set as INVITE_DOMAIN.

A message arriving at an address that does not belong to anyone, or one that is not a booking confirmation, is refused with the reason, so the sender sees it. Forwarded confirmations are held for the advisor to check and are never turned into reservations without them, and a held message is deleted when it is used, dismissed, or after 45 days.

Is it still running

  • Background jobs. The portal does a dozen things on a schedule: payment reminders, call lists, digests, the lifecycle sweep. The card says when each last ran and when it last ran without failing. Those two dates being far apart is a job that has quietly stopped working, which is otherwise invisible, because a reminder nobody gets looks exactly like a reminder nobody needed.
  • Database. Whether the live database has had every change applied. Changes ship in the code and are applied to the database by hand, so the two can drift. When they do, the page that reads the missing column returns an error and this card names the file to run. Worth a look after anything is released.
  • Mail. Whether a mail key is set and whether the sending domain is verified. A sending service will not send from a domain it has not verified, and it fails without an error anybody sees. A send with no mail key waits rather than marking everybody as failed, so nothing is lost while it is sorted out.

What you can see, and what you cannot do

You see the whole agency wherever a screen offers a scope picker, and you can narrow it to one advisor. The calendar is the exception in how it opens: it starts on your own diary and the list at the top widens it to everybody or one advisor. That includes leads, marketing and the tiles across the top of a page, and the trips an advisor has put together from several reservations. Two things never widen, whatever the picker says:

Every screen opens on your own book, and the choice follows you. Pick another advisor, or All advisors, in the list at the top of any page and the next page you open keeps it, until you change it. You do not have to pick yourself again on each screen.

  • Writes stay with the person who owns the record. Seeing an advisor's reservation is not the same as editing it as yourself.
  • A marketing email goes out as one person, to that person's own clients. There is no button that mails the whole agency's book in one go, on purpose: a client gave their address to their advisor, not to the agency's list.

Something here wrong, or missing? It gets fixed in the portal rather than in a document nobody reads.
